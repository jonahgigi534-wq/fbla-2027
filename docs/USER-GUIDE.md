# User guide

The same guides are readable inside the program under **Help**, which is where to look
during a demonstration.

## Ordering

**Pick a restaurant first.** The home screen lists all six with a live open or closed
badge. Hours differ: Fuqua never closes, Katy runs around the clock at weekends, and
the other four run 7:00 AM to midnight. Delivery areas differ too, so the choice
changes what you can order and when you can collect it.

**Find something.** The menu holds 426 items across 34 categories.

- The search box matches item names first and ingredients second, so searching
  `pecan` puts the pecan pies above a burger that happens to mention pecans.
- Section tabs narrow to Food, Bakery, Drinks, A la Carte, or Catering.
- Chips filter to vegetarian, vegan, sugar free, seasonal, or only what is available.
- Sort by best match, price either way, or name.

**Add it.** Open an item to see the price, ingredients, dietary tags, and allergens.
Choose a quantity and add it. The stepper will not go past what the restaurant has
left, and it says how many that is.

If something is sold out it stays on the menu, greyed out, and its page offers three
alternatives from the same part of the menu at a similar price.

**Set a spending limit, if you want one.** In your order, enter what you want to stay
under. The limit is measured against the full total including tax, because that is
the figure that leaves your account. You get a warning as you approach it, and if you
pass it checkout is blocked and the cheapest item that would bring you back under is
named.

**Choose how you want it.** Pickup, delivery, or dine in. Delivery orders start at
$15.00, and the fee is waived over $35.00.

**Enter a promo code, if you have one.** Codes are listed in the assistant under
"Any discounts?". The discount comes off before tax is worked out.

**Check out.** You will be asked for a name, phone number, email, and a collection
time. Delivery orders also ask for an address, and pickup orders do not, because
asking for something and then ignoring it teaches people to distrust a form.

Every field is checked when you leave it. Pressing Place order then runs the checks
that need the whole order, such as whether the restaurant delivers to your ZIP and
whether your collection time is far enough out for what is in the cart.

**No payment is taken.** Nothing is sent anywhere and no card number is stored. Only
the last four digits reach the saved order so a receipt can identify the card.

## After ordering

**Follow it.** Orders move through Received, Baking, Ready, and Complete.

**Change or cancel it.** While an order is still Received you can change any
quantity on it, or cancel it outright. Once the kitchen starts, neither is offered:
the ingredients are committed whatever anyone decides. Canceling puts the stock back.

**Print a receipt.** The print view strips the navigation and buttons out.

**Review your spending.** The Your spending screen totals what you have spent over 30,
90, or 365 days and names what you order most, with a button to order it again.

## Asking the assistant

Press **Ask** in the header. It answers from this device, with no internet, reading
the live menu, stock, cart, and orders.

Things it handles: what is vegetarian, anything under ten dollars, what do you
recommend, what is sold out, when are you open, do you deliver, where is my order,
what is in my cart, what has nuts in it, do you do catering, any discounts, how do I
order, where are you, can I set a spending limit.

It copes with typos, and if it does not understand it offers the nearest topics rather
than giving up. The buttons under the box are questions guaranteed to work.

## The staff area

Press **Staff**. The PIN is **1967**, printed on the screen that asks for it because
this is a demonstration and a hidden PIN would lock the audience out of half the
program. It is not security and does not pretend to be.

**Order queue.** Orders placed in this session, newest first. One button moves a
ticket to its next stage. The ninety days of past orders are not here; they are what
the reports are for.

**Inventory.** Search or filter to what is running low. Change any stock number, or
mark something sold out. The change takes effect on the customer side immediately:
the item greys out on the menu, refuses to go into a cart, and starts offering
substitutes.

**Reports.** Choose a date range, filter by restaurant or order type, group seven
ways, and measure four ways. Every column heading sorts by it.

Above the table: four headline figures with the same figures from the period before,
and sentences naming what actually moved. Below it: export to CSV, or print.

**Reset demo data** returns everything to a fresh install and rebuilds the ninety days
of history. Worth doing between demonstrations.
